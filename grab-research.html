Understand the system behind the brand.

The research started as curiosity about Grab and became a structured effort to understand how company history, products, platform economics, mapping, users and engineering footprint fit together.

03 · VOICE OF CUSTOMER

Reviews are signals, not root causes.

I grouped recurring complaints across consumer, merchant, driver and mapping/data-contributor perspectives. The strongest value of public reviews is showing repeated user-perceived problems; they do not reveal internal technical causes on their own.

04 · GRABMAPS

The mapping model was the part most connected to my own project curiosity.

I studied GrabMaps' community-based mapping model, service APIs and mapmaking tools. That research also helped me understand why hyperlocal mapping depends on much more than drawing a route on a screen.

05 · METHOD

Independent student research, not professional experience at Grab.

The work combines company sources, public product information, secondary sources and public reviews. AI helped organize and synthesize parts of the research, while interesting claims were separately checked when they mattered.

06 · LIMITATIONS

Public evidence has a boundary.

App reviews are self-selected and cannot represent all Grab users. Public documentation cannot prove internal motives, technical root causes or unpublished product decisions. The case study therefore separates observed complaints from hypotheses about why they may occur.

What further evidence would help

Telemetry, internal product data, user interviews, experiments, operational evidence and engineering investigation.
