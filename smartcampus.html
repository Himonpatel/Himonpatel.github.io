01 · PROBLEM

Navigation alone was not enough.

The project began as an indoor campus-navigation idea. After faculty feedback, the scope expanded into a broader operations layer where room allocation, class schedules, faculty changes, student notifications and campus navigation can respond to one another.

Core product question

What should happen when a room, class or campus resource changes after the timetable has already been created?

03 · MY ROLE

Product and system thinking came before implementation.

As team leader, I focused heavily on product scope, cross-role workflows, navigation-data reasoning, testing strategy and the decisions needed to make the prototype coherent. I also implemented and debugged substantial backend and integration work with AI assistance. I do not present the entire team codebase as if I personally wrote every line.

04 · TESTING

A larger dataset exposed the weakness behind a convincing interface.

I moved toward a more structured synthetic campus dataset so expected room relationships, occupancy and routes could be checked rather than merely demonstrated. The route flow exposed that the spatial model still needs stronger architectural detail and clearer room / door / node relationships.

What improved

The data model and visual representation are more structured than the original dummy-data version.

What the test changed

I stopped treating a visible route line as evidence that indoor navigation was good enough.

05 · VALIDATION BOUNDARY

A prototype can prove the flow without proving real-world navigation accuracy.

Controlled synthetic data can test whether system logic behaves as expected. Real positioning accuracy, architectural-grade mapping and deployment on an actual campus remain separate validation steps.

Current description

Working academic prototype — not a claim of production deployment.
